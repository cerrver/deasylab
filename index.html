Why our work matters

Turning cancer's variability into better treatment

Cancer varies — from patient to patient, tumor to tumor, and cell to cell. The Deasy Lab treats that variability as information: we build models that read tumor biology from routinely acquired images, genomes, and clinical data, and turn it into decisions that make treatment more precise and less toxic. The aim is not modeling for its own sake, but tools that reach the clinic and change how patients are cared for.

Consortium

VALIDATE: putting radiotherapy prediction models to the test

Chaired by Dr. Deasy

VALIdation of Decision support models for rAdioThErapy

Most published radiotherapy outcome models are never tested on patients they have not seen. VALIDATE is a multi-institutional consortium of 27 investigators in ten countries that runs rigorous external validations of published outcome and decision-support models: the analysis plan is locked before any data are touched, every model is tested at three or more institutions independent of its developer, and both discrimination and calibration are reported. The code travels; patient data stay where they were collected. Dr. Deasy chairs the consortium; Laura Cella (CNR, Naples) co-chairs.

Research programs

Four connected lines of work

The through-line across all of them: innovative mathematics used to understand patient and disease variability — and to turn it into a leverage point for cancer care.

01

Mathematical Oncology Initiative

Geometry & optimal transport for the structure of cancer

Breast Cancer Research Foundation · Simons Foundation

Geometry- and topology-based methods to subtype patients, integrate multi-omic networks, and surface therapeutic targets — and to train the next generation who build them.

optimal transportnetwork curvatureNetFlow
02

ECHO

Automated, clinically optimal treatment planning

2021 INFORMS Franz Edelman Laureate

A prioritized optimization that turns the hand-tuned trade-off between tumor coverage and organ sparing into a reproducible, mathematically optimal plan — now used to help plan roughly 23% of patients treated at MSK.

03

Proton & particle radiobiology

Modeling response across radiation modalities

A thread since the lab's first NIH award

Mechanistic models of how dose, timing, and tumor biology combine to determine response and toxicity when the physics differs from conventional photons — reproducing clinical observations across photon and carbon-ion therapy.

04

Digital twins

A simulatable model of each patient's tumor

Multimodality imaging + tumor biology

Combining FDG-PET and MRI with a mechanistic tumor-control model to forecast response and flag likely non-responders early — while treatment can still be adapted.

CERR / pyCERR

The lab's open-source platform for relating medical images to radiotherapy dose and outcomes — registration, dose calculation, contouring, and quantitative ("radiomic") image analysis. CERR has underpinned 70+ of the lab's studies and been cited over 800 times; pyCERR is its native Python successor, with a graphical viewer and AI-pipeline utilities. Development is led by Aditya Apte, PhD.

AI auto-segmentation models

Deep-learning models for segmenting tumors and organs at risk on CT and MRI — heart and cardiac substructures, lung, head-and-neck, prostate, rectum, pancreas, and whole-body models — published with pyCERR and installable with one command via the CERR model installer. Models of this kind are in routine clinical use for radiotherapy planning at MSK.

ORCO

Ollivier-Ricci Curvature-Omics: an open-source Python package that lays omics data over gene- or protein-interaction networks and measures the curvature of each interaction — a geometric readout of how robust or fragile a biological system is, which can surface hidden therapeutic targets and regulatory modules. Install with pip install orcomics.

NetFlow

A framework for exploring topological representations of high-dimensional biomedical data. NetFlow turns a cohort of samples into an interpretable graph of sample relatedness — built on optimal transport, diffusion, and topological data analysis — to support precision oncology and hypothesis generation, with Jupyter tutorials that reproduce the multiple-myeloma analysis from the paper.

PythonTopology · OMTPreprint 2025
Documentation → GitHub → Preprint →

ViSPACE

Virchow2-powered Spatial Characterization and feature Extraction. An end-to-end computational pathology pipeline for whole-slide images of lung and breast cancer: it pairs the Virchow2 pathology foundation model with a pixel-wise decoder to segment tissue into tumor, stroma, inflammatory cells (TILs), necrosis and other, then extracts quantitative spatial biomarkers of the tumor microenvironment — tumor–stroma ratio, stromal TIL scores, immune and necrosis proximity, and tumor morphology. Developed by Himangi Srivastava; open source (Apache 2.0), paper under submission.

Discovery

A high-risk breast-cancer subtype found by computation

Using optimal-transport-based clustering of molecular data, the lab isolated a poor-prognosis, hypoxia-enriched subgroup of breast cancers and characterized its biology — showing that geometry-based methods can define clinically meaningful subtypes that conventional analyses miss.

Oh et al., npj Breast Cancer (2025), doi:10.1038/s41523-025-00725-y.
