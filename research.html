Research

Mathematics as a leverage point for cancer care

Four connected programs, one through-line: innovative mathematical models that turn patient and disease variability into more precise, less toxic treatment.

Program 01 · Mathematical Oncology Initiative

Geometry and optimal transport for the structure of cancer

Multimodal cancer datasets — genomics, computational pathology, radiomics — are high-dimensional and noisy, and conventional correlation-based analyses poorly capture the geometric structure needed to define clinically meaningful cancer subtypes. The Mathematical Oncology Initiative applies advanced, innovative mathematics to this problem, adapting optimal mass transport (OMT) and graph-based methods to cluster samples, integrate multi-omic networks, and surface therapeutic targets.

The initiative has a deliberate second goal: to mentor and train talented fellows and junior faculty in these methods. It is supported by the Breast Cancer Research Foundation and the Simons Foundation.

Patient subtyping

OMT / Wasserstein-distance clustering to define subtypes across breast, ovarian, sarcoma, melanoma, and lung cancers.

Network geometry

Curvature and geometric analysis of gene-interaction networks to find fragile points and candidate drug targets.

Target discovery

Hypothesis generation — e.g., pregnancy-specific glycoproteins as candidate lung-cancer drug targets.

Program 02 · ECHO

Automated, clinically optimal treatment planning

Intensity-modulated radiotherapy planning is a high-dimensional optimization problem (more than 10,000 variables) that trades tumor coverage against sparing many normal tissues. Conventional weighted-sum methods give planners poor, unintuitive control over the resulting trade-offs. ECHO (Expedited Constrained Hierarchical Optimization) solves the most clinically critical objectives first, then admits lower-priority objectives as constraints that preserve what has already been achieved — yielding explicit, reproducible, clinically deliverable plans.

ECHO is now used to help plan roughly 23% of all patients treated at MSK and was recognized with 2021 INFORMS Franz Edelman Laureate honors for impact in applied analytics. It was refined and implemented clinically with optimization expert Masoud Zarepisheh, PhD.

Program 03 · Proton & particle radiobiology

Modeling response across radiation modalities

Proton and particle therapies deposit dose with a physics profile very different from conventional photons, which changes how tumors and healthy tissues respond. The lab builds mechanistic models of how dose, timing, and tumor biology combine to determine response and toxicity across modalities; our tumor-control models reproduce key clinical observations under both photon and carbon-ion irradiation, giving a quantitative basis for comparing modalities and personalizing prescriptions. Program 04 · Digital twins

A simulatable model of each patient's tumor

A "digital twin" is a computational model of an individual patient's tumor that can be simulated forward in time. Building on a parsimonious, mechanistic "energy-budget" model of tumor control developed with Jeho Jeong, the lab combines multimodality imaging (FDG-PET and MRI) with tumor biology to forecast how a given tumor will respond to radiation — and to flag likely non-responders early, while treatment can still be adapted. Related efforts synthesize hypoxia information from routine PET and forecast normal-tissue toxicity at the level of individual image voxels.
